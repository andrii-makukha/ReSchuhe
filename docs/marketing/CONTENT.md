# ReSchuhe Content Standard

Status: Governance confirmed; message, voice, and languages pending | Last reviewed: 2026-08-07

## Current state

The product offering, audience, brand promise, tone of voice, and launch locales are not yet approved. Therefore, no sample slogan, marketing claim, or feature copy is canonical.

## Content principles

- Lead with the information a user needs to decide or act.
- Use concrete, plain language and explain necessary specialist terms.
- Make headings, labels, and links meaningful outside their surrounding paragraph.
- State conditions, limitations, price implications, and uncertainty where relevant.
- Support factual and comparative claims with current evidence and an owner.
- Do not manufacture urgency, scarcity, endorsements, reviews, or social proof.
- Keep instructions and errors actionable without blaming the user.
- Write for localization and real content variation; do not rely on fixed string length.

## Content hierarchy

For each page or journey, define:

1. the user question the content answers;
2. the primary message;
3. evidence needed to support it;
4. the next meaningful action;
5. secondary detail and disclosure;
6. ownership, review date, and expiry conditions.

## UI copy requirements

- Buttons describe the action and expected outcome.
- Links describe the destination.
- Form labels remain visible; placeholders are examples, not labels.
- Help text appears before an avoidable error.
- Error text names the problem and a recovery step without exposing sensitive detail.
- Success text confirms what happened and what happens next.
- Empty states explain the state and offer a relevant path forward when one exists.

## Content lifecycle

Draft content from confirmed requirements, verify claims and legal implications, review voice and accessibility, approve an owner and review date, publish through the selected content workflow, and retire or revise stale material.

Do not select a CMS merely to begin drafting. The CMS decision follows the confirmed content model, permissions, frequency, preview, localization, and publishing needs.

## Public release content gate

The Public Release Slice uses real approved content for its confirmed market and audience. It must
not present planned capabilities as available, use fabricated proof, or rely on placeholder copy for
critical decisions. Every public claim, support path, policy link, and review date needs an owner.

## Open decisions

- Primary audience and reading context.
- Brand promise, message hierarchy, vocabulary, formality, and prohibited language.
- Launch languages and source language.
- Content types, fields, owners, approval workflow, and review cadence.
- Claims evidence and legal review responsibility.
- Human-authorship and AI-assistance disclosure policy.
